Course project · Team build

Building a catapult.

A classroom challenge to launch a tennis ball as far as possible—with a limited budget and a design we could build ourselves.

Project setting
Engineering graphics & design
Budget limit
$100
Build approach
Mechanical power · Common hand tools

The challenge

Our team was asked to design and build a catapult that could launch a tennis ball as far as possible. The project had a $100 budget and required mechanical power. Pneumatic, hydraulic, and explosive systems were outside the project rules.

We also needed a design that could be made with the hand tools and materials available to us.

From ideas to a build

We researched different catapult designs, combined ideas, and sketched a plan. The design used wood, piping, and bungee cords, balancing the launch concept with what we could realistically build.

I worked with the team through planning and construction. We coordinated access to tools and resources, worked through the assembly, and completed the build within two weeks.

Testing & reflection

We carried the project through to launch trials. The process showed me how much a physical build depends on planning the details, coordinating with teammates, and responding to constraints as they come up.

It was an early opportunity to connect an engineering idea to something tangible—from a sketch and a parts list to an assembled mechanism.
