ME 347 · Design & prototyping

15:1 gear-ratio fidget.

A mechanical fidget that took me from hand sketches and CAD concepts to a printed prototype, with practical lessons about how gears fit and engage.

Course
ME 347
Design target
15:1 gear ratio
Process
Sketching · CAD · 3D printing

The idea

For my ME 347 class, I developed a mechanical fidget with a 15:1 gear-ratio design target. The project explored a hand-operated mechanism housed in a compact case.

Developing the design

I started with hand sketches to explore different ways to drive the gears, including a lever and a rotating handle. I then developed CAD prototypes to work through the mechanism and how its parts would fit inside the housing.

The final design used a circular hand input and a series of gears in a rectangular case. I moved from the CAD assembly to a 3D-printed prototype so I could examine how the physical parts fit and operated.

What the prototype revealed

The build showed how sensitive the mechanism was to gear alignment and clearance. Applying too much force by hand caused the drive gear’s small teeth to skip; it required a lighter input to keep the gears engaged.

In my project reflection, I identified more precise tolerances, checking gear alignment before printing, and larger drive-gear teeth as areas to address in a future revision.

What I learned

Moving from CAD to a physical mechanism made the fit between components much more concrete. The prototype gave me specific feedback about tooth engagement and clearances to carry into the next design iteration.
