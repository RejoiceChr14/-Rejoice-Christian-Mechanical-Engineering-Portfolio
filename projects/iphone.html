Personal project · Repair

Inside the iPhone.

Repairing old phones gave me a closer look at the hardware I used every day—and a practical reason to learn how it all fits together.

Devices
iPhone 5 & iPhone 6
My role
Independent repair project
Focus
Troubleshooting & component replacement

Why I started

I wanted to understand what was inside an iPhone and try repairing devices instead of replacing them. Working on older iPhone 5 and iPhone 6 models gave me a way to explore the components, connections, and assembly firsthand.

Two phones, different problems

For the iPhone 6, I disassembled the device, ordered a replacement screen, and installed it. The repair required careful handling of small components and attention to how the screen connected to the rest of the phone.

The iPhone 5 had a motherboard issue associated with a boot loop, along with a dead battery. I used a motherboard and battery from another badly damaged iPhone 5 as donor parts to keep the repair cost down.

What I took away

The project gave me practice with careful disassembly, component replacement, and reassembly. It also pushed me to connect a visible problem with the hardware that might be causing it.

Seeing the arrangement of the internal parts made the engineering behind everyday electronics more tangible. That experience encouraged me to keep exploring repair and engineering projects.
