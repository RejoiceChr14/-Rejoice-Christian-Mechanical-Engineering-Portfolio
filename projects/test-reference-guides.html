The laboratory problem

Some fluids-lab tests were performed only a few times each year. Between those tests, practical setup knowledge could become difficult to retain. Newer technicians had to work through the associated standard and Test Data Sheets to understand the setup, and those documents did not always make every potential risk clear.

My contribution

During my UL Solutions internship, I developed reference guides for six of these tests. I brought together the associated UL standards, Test Data Sheets used for sample testing, and the knowledge and guidance of lab technicians.

What each guide covered

  1. Equipment

    The equipment required for the test, organized so technicians could prepare for the setup.

  2. Setup

    A clearer explanation of how the test was arranged, informed by technician experience as well as written references.

  3. Procedure

    The sequence and supporting information needed to understand how the test was performed.

  4. Potential risks

    Test-related risks that needed to be made explicit alongside the setup and procedure.

The deliverable and its purpose

The resulting digital materials put the key information into a consistent reference format. The goal was to make setup information easier to find, reduce the knowledge gaps around infrequent tests, and retain practical knowledge across the engineering and laboratory teams.

What I learned

I learned to connect formal technical requirements with the practical questions technicians encounter. The work strengthened my technical writing, standards research, and ability to gather useful information from experienced colleagues.

This overview respects my confidentiality obligations. Internal guides, test identities, detailed procedures, and company materials are not published.
